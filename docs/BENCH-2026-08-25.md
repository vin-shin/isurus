# Bench session — 2026-08-25

Simulator-versus-hardware validation of the current loop, run at 22.2 V with
the motor connected. Every number here came off the bench or out of
`test/host/sim_dump` on the same day; the figures are regenerable rather than
committed, and the commands to rebuild each one are given below.

Firmware under test: `09d54ed` (unchanged from the previous session — the
naming work touched no code on the ISR path).

## The model is validated at two operating points

This is the result worth keeping. `foc.c` compiled natively against the PMSM
model, laid over the same firmware running on silicon:

| | sim peak \|id\| | hw peak \|id\| |
|---|---|---|
| 400→1200, decouple=1 | 37 mA | 105 mA |
| 400→1200, decouple=0 | 102 mA | 245 mA |
| 400→2400, decouple=1 | 93 mA | 106 mA |
| 400→2400, decouple=0 | 311 mA | 336 mA |

At the higher current the agreement is close to exact — the simulator peaks
`id` at +0.33 A through the step and the bench ensemble mean at ~+0.35 A, with
the same decay. Agreement is worse at 1200 mA for a reason that is not model
error: the hardware carries roughly ±0.4 A of ripple that the model has no
harmonics to produce, and at the smaller step that ripple is a larger fraction
of the disturbance being measured. The model gets better where the signal is
bigger, which is the right direction for the HV work.

**The decoupling benefit and its trend both reproduce:**

| step | sim | hardware |
|---|---|---|
| 400 → 1200 mA | −64% peak | **−57% peak**, −55% rms |
| 400 → 2400 mA | −70% peak | **−68% peak**, −59% rms |

The benefit grows with current in both, which is what `ω_e·Lq·iq` predicts: a
bigger `iq` step is a bigger cross-coupling disturbance for the feedforward to
cancel.

## An inductance hypothesis, tested and refuted

Worth recording because the wrong answer was superficially convincing.

The bench current loop reaches 63% of a step in **64 ± 29 µs** against the
simulator's **200 µs**. A real inductance below the assumed `FOC_L_H` would do
exactly that, and prompt.md section E already names characterising Lq as the
largest open risk for the HV build — so the hypothesis was that the bench
motor's L is well under 54.3 µH.

An `L_err=` option was added to `sim_dump` (mirroring the existing
`lambda_err=`) to scale the **model's** Ld/Lq while leaving the firmware's
constants alone — a plant that is wrong by that factor while the controller
still designs for 54.3 µH. Two things then appeared to support the hypothesis:

- rise time fell to the hardware's ~64 µs at `L_err ≈ 3`;
- peak \|id\| with decoupling on rose from 37 mA to **103 mA** at `L_err = 3`,
  against a bench measurement of **105 mA**.

Both are worthless. The rise time saturates: 67 µs is two control periods, so
a loop settling inside two periods reads the same whether the truth is 67 µs
or 20, and the bench sits on that same floor. And the 103-vs-105 mA agreement
was coincidence — at `L_err = 3` the *other* arm disagrees completely, 48 mA
simulated against 245 measured. A mis-tuned feedforward leaves a residue about
the size of a correct one, so the ON number alone cannot tell them apart.

The **benefit** — OFF against ON — is diagnostic, because it changes sign:

| model L | sim benefit, 400→1200 | sim benefit, 400→2400 |
|---|---|---|
| 54.3 µH (nominal) | −64% | −70% |
| 27.1 µH | **+91%** | **+25%** |
| 18.1 µH | **+113%** | **+85%** |

Below about 30 µH the feedforward over-corrects and the "correction" makes the
disturbance worse. The bench never does this — it shows −57% and −68%, and
both simulator curves cross the measured values at 54.3 µH. **The bench motor
behaves like a motor whose inductance is the one the firmware already
assumes.** The hypothesis is refuted.

The rise-time gap is therefore still unexplained, and it is not inductance.
Candidates not yet excluded: the model's 1.5-period transport delay against
the firmware's 1.65 (prompt.md already flags this), and alignment bias in the
ensemble mean, since `viz.py` locates the step per capture and averaging
traces aligned on their own crossing sharpens the transition.

## Phase 1a cannot be validated on this bench

The `delay_comp` A/B measured +38% peak \|id\| with compensation on — which
reads as "the correction is harmful" and means nothing. The difference is
46 mA against noise floors of 29.5 and 46.6 mA on the averaged waveform: about
one noise unit, at 12 reps.

The simulator says how big the effect actually is: **−9 mA, −7%** on peak
\|id\|. Against those floors, a 2σ read needs

    ~514 reps   from the 29.5 mA floor
    ~1282 reps  from the 46.6 mA floor

So this is not an underpowered measurement that more patience fixes — it is
infeasible by this method on this bench. That is the same conclusion prompt.md
already records for 1a ("stands on derivation"), now with a rep count behind
it. The compensation *is* acting: +5.31° of advance applied against +0.00°
with it off.

Anything that wants to measure 1a needs a lower noise floor, not more reps —
a locked rotor, or a disturbance injected at a known frequency and detected
narrowband.

## Two tool bugs found

**`viz.py select()` matched `--hw-key` against any field.** A capture is named
`hw <flag> <rep>`, and the test was `key in t.name.split()`, so `--hw-key 1`
also matched rep 1 of the *other* arm — a 12-rep A/B plotted as 13 captures
with one from the wrong firmware configuration. Exactly the blend the
function's own docstring calls worse than not comparing at all, arriving
silently. Visible in the operating point too: the contaminated average
reported ω_e 1648 rad/s against the simulator's 1670; matching the flag field
alone puts it at 1669. Fixed in `d8b1239`.

**`step_trace.sh` does not restore the flags it toggles.** It safes the power
stage on exit — that part works — but leaves `f->decouple` at whichever arm
ran last. Observed twice today: `decouple` read 1 after both A/B runs, against
a firmware default of 0 (`foc.c:241`). A reset clears it, and nothing here was
measured through a stale flag because each run sets both arms explicitly. The
hazard is what runs *next*: `foc_dash --demo` immediately after a step trace
would spin with decoupling silently enabled, which is the configuration
prompt.md says costs 6× peak current on a reversal. Not fixed — the tool
should snapshot and restore, but that is a change to a motor-driving script
and wants its own review.

## Also noted

- `foc.h:381` comments `decouple` as *"(default on)"*; `foc.c:241` sets it to
  `0`. The comment is stale.
- `viz.py live` plots the integer mirrors, which only update while the control
  loop runs. Against a drive sitting in `READY` it draws a flat line from
  whenever the loop last ran — it looks like data. Worth a guard that reads
  `g_foc.updates` twice and refuses rather than plotting a frozen snapshot.
- `CSense_MeasureVdda` runs once at startup and latches. One boot in seven
  read VREFINT 1728 against a stable 1522, putting VDDA at 2890 mV instead of
  3280 and every derived voltage 13% low for the whole session. With
  `vbus_track = 1` that mis-scales the current-loop gains too. `vbus_raw` was
  unchanged across the bad boot, which is what rules out an actual rail sag:
  the ADC is ratiometric, so a real 2.89 V rail would have moved the raw code.

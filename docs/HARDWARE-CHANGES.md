# GR MotherFOCer — hardware changes worth making

Findings from the firmware port that are hardware questions, not firmware
ones. Everything here came out of reading `powerunit.pdf`, the TL200 datasheet
and the CubeMX project against what the EMRAX 228 HV actually needs.

Ordered by what they buy, not by effort.

---

## 1. Connect the fifteen fast protection signals — recommended

There are **fifteen hardware protection signals on this board that no
firmware can see.** Every one of them is already generated by a part that is
already fitted. They are not connected to anything that acts on them.

### 1a. The sensors' OCD outputs — three pins

Each Mornsun TL200-A2PV brings out an over-current detect pin (pin 1). It
goes high when the primary current passes **±400 A**, and on this board it
goes nowhere.

That threshold is well chosen without anybody having chosen it:

| | |
|---|---|
| motor peak, 240 Arms | 339 A — below the trip, so it cannot nuisance trip |
| **OCD trip** | **400 A** |
| sensor measurement range | 500 A — above the trip, so the reading is still valid when it fires |

It is also a comparator *inside* the sensor, so it responds in the 0.3 µs the
datasheet quotes for a current step. The firmware current limit reacts at
20 kHz — a 50 µs period — and only to current the ADC has already digitised.
For a short-circuit or a shoot-through, 0.3 µs versus 50 µs is the difference
between a fault and a destroyed bridge.

**What to do:** route all three OCD pins to the MCU. They can be OR'd into a
single input if pins are short, though losing which phase complained costs
real diagnostic value. Any EXTI-capable pin works; the handler calls
`MotorPwm_EmergencyStop()`, which is already written to run from a broken
context using single-store register writes.

### 1b. The gate drivers' fault and ready lines — twelve pins, already wired

These *are* connected — to the MCU, on the twelve pins this port spent weeks
calling "unexplained inputs":

| | ready | fault | | ready | fault |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| U high | PB4 | PD2 | V high | PC9 | PA8 |
| U low | PA9 | PA12 | V low | PB14 | PB15 |
| W high | PB0 | PB11 | W low | PB10 | PB2 |

So this one is **not a hardware change at all** — it is firmware that has not
been written. The drivers are reporting desaturation, loss of isolated supply
and thermal shutdown, per switch, and the pins sit in their reset state with
nobody listening. A fault line read as a floating input is not a fault handled
badly; it is a fault that never happened as far as the firmware is concerned.

**What is needed before it can be written** is a hardware answer, which is why
it is in this document: the **gate driver part number**, for

- the polarity of each line, and whether READY asserts on healthy or not-ready
- whether FAULT latches in the driver until `DRV_RST` (PC8) is pulsed, which
  decides whether firmware may clear it or must reset the driver
- whether they are open-drain, which decides whether they need pulls

## 2. The current sensor is fine — an earlier recommendation withdrawn

An earlier version of this port recommended replacing the TL200-A2PV with a
±350 A part. **That was wrong and should not be acted on.**

The "200" in the part number is IPN, the *effective* range — the current at
which the output reaches its ±0.625 V full-scale figure. Every part in the
family does that at its own IPN (6.25 mV/A × 100, 4.167 × 150, 3.125 × 200).
The **measurement range is ±500 A**, against a motor peak of 339 A.

So the binding limit on current is the motor, not the sensor, which is the
right way round. There is nothing to change.

## 3. CAN FD — no hardware change needed, and it will not buy precision

**The board is already CAN FD capable.** The transceiver is a **TCAN1044A**
(sheet 7), which is a CAN FD part, and FDCAN2 on the STM32G474 is an FD
controller. Firmware currently sets `FDCAN_FRAME_CLASSIC` in `can.c`; enabling
FD is that line plus a data-phase bit timing set. **Do not add a second
transceiver.**

### It will not improve telemetry precision, because the wire is not the limit

This is worth being precise about, because it is the stated reason for asking.
Telemetry resolution is currently finer than the measurements it carries:

| quantity | the hardware resolves | the wire carries |
|---|---|---|
| phase current | 314 mA per ADC count | 100 mA (deciamps) |
| bus voltage | ~320 mV per ADC count | 10 mV (centivolts) |
| position | 4 counts of 32768 (13-bit encoder, widened) | 0.1° |

Widening those fields would report more digits of numbers that are not known
to that many digits. If telemetry precision is genuinely the goal, the levers
are the **encoder** (13 bits, and its bottom 2 reported bits are always zero)
and the **ADC** (12-bit, ~314 mA per count on current) — not the bus.

### What CAN FD would actually buy

Three real things, none of which is precision:

1. **Payload space.** 64 bytes against 8. Right now every telemetry frame is
   full, and the channels this port has added — winding temperature, three
   power-stage temperatures, DC link current, and the twelve gate-driver fault
   lines once they are read — have nowhere to go without minting new frame
   IDs. That is the strongest argument.
2. **Lower bus load.** A higher data-phase rate means the same telemetry costs
   less of a shared bus, or the same bus carries a higher telemetry rate.
3. **Fewer frames**, so less arbitration contention.

### What it would cost

- **Every other node on the bus must tolerate FD frames.** A classic-only
  controller does not simply ignore an FD frame — it can error on it and
  disturb the bus. This is a whole-vehicle decision, not an inverter one.
- Two bit-timing sets to get right instead of one, and the data phase is where
  a marginal bus shows up first.
- Check the competition rules before committing.

The e-stop design survives unchanged: a broadcast at identifier `0x000` still
wins arbitration, because arbitration happens in the classic-format header.

**Recommendation:** worth doing, for payload rather than precision, and only
once the rest of the bus is known to be FD-tolerant. It is a firmware change
whenever you want it — there is no hardware to buy.

## 4. Dead time — re-derive it, do not assume it carried over

Not a change to make, a number to confirm, and it is here because the answer
comes from a datasheet rather than from the firmware.

The HRTIM dead time is **160 counts at DT prescaler DIV1**, which at
fDTG = fHRTIM = 160 MHz is **1.0 µs**. That was inherited from
`gr_motherfocer`, whose hardware is reported identical **except for the
FETs** — and dead time is precisely a FET property: it covers the device's
turn-off delay plus the mismatch between the two gate-driver channels.

1.0 µs is also long for a MOSFET bridge, where 100–500 ns is typical.

It is being left long on purpose, because the error directions are not
symmetric. Too long costs modulation range and adds distortion near the zero
crossing. **Too short is a shoot-through** — one the firmware cannot detect,
cannot fault on, and which damages the bridge cumulatively.

**What to do:** re-derive from the new FETs' turn-off characteristics, then
confirm on a scope at a switch node before the bridge sees any real bus
voltage. Do not shorten it on a datasheet alone.

## 5. Open questions that need a person, not a document

Neither of these is answerable from any schematic, and both gate closed-loop
operation:

1. **Two known bus voltages and their ADC codes.** The bus sense is a 400:1
   divider into an AMC0311 isolated amplifier into an MCP6496 difference
   amplifier. Firmware models the divider alone and assumes zero offset. Two
   points give gain and offset directly, and measure what the board does
   rather than what three parts in series should do.

2. **Phase sense polarity, per phase.** The conditioning is now known not to
   invert, so any sign flip is the sensor's own convention plus which way the
   conductor passes through it. A reversed phase sensor does not fail loudly —
   it corrupts the Clarke transform into a rotating error.

Both are one bench session, and the same session that answers §5.1 can answer
§5.2.

the motor connected. Every number here came off the bench or out of

naming work touched no code on the ISR path).

## The model is validated at two operating points

This is the result worth keeping. `foc.c` compiled natively against the PMSM
model, laid over the same firmware running on silicon:

| | sim peak \|id\| | hw peak \|id\| |
|---|---|---|
| 400→1200, decouple=1 | 37 mA | 105 mA |
| 400→1200, decouple=0 | 102 mA | 245 mA |
| 400→2400, decouple=1 | 93 mA | 106 mA |
| 400→2400, decouple=0 | 311 mA | 336 mA |

At the higher current the agreement is close to exact — the simulator peaks
`id` at +0.33 A through the step and the bench ensemble mean at ~+0.35 A, with
the same decay. Agreement is worse at 1200 mA for a reason that is not model
error: the hardware carries roughly ±0.4 A of ripple that the model has no
harmonics to produce, and at the smaller step that ripple is a larger fraction
of the disturbance being measured. The model gets better where the signal is
bigger, which is the right direction for the HV work.

**The decoupling benefit and its trend both reproduce:**

| step | sim | hardware |
|---|---|---|
| 400 → 1200 mA | −64% peak | **−57% peak**, −55% rms |
| 400 → 2400 mA | −70% peak | **−68% peak**, −59% rms |

The benefit grows with current in both, which is what `ω_e·Lq·iq` predicts: a
bigger `iq` step is a bigger cross-coupling disturbance for the feedforward to
cancel.

## An inductance hypothesis, tested and refuted

Worth recording because the wrong answer was superficially convincing.

The bench current loop appeared to reach 63% of a step in **64 µs** against
the simulator's **200 µs**. A real inductance below the assumed `FOC_L_H`
would do exactly that, and prompt.md section E already names characterising Lq
as the largest open risk for the HV build — so the hypothesis was that the
bench motor's L is well under 54.3 µH.

**That 3× gap was not real** — see the section below. It was a plotting-tool
artifact, found after this hypothesis had already been refuted on independent
grounds. The refutation stands on its own and is worth reading anyway, because
the way it failed is instructive.

An `L_err=` option was added to `sim_dump` (mirroring the existing
`lambda_err=`) to scale the **model's** Ld/Lq while leaving the firmware's
constants alone — a plant that is wrong by that factor while the controller
still designs for 54.3 µH. Two things then appeared to support the hypothesis:

- rise time fell to the hardware's ~64 µs at `L_err ≈ 3`;
- peak \|id\| with decoupling on rose from 37 mA to **103 mA** at `L_err = 3`,
  against a bench measurement of **105 mA**.

Both are worthless. The rise time saturates: 67 µs is two control periods, so
a loop settling inside two periods reads the same whether the truth is 67 µs
or 20, and the bench sits on that same floor. And the 103-vs-105 mA agreement
was coincidence — at `L_err = 3` the *other* arm disagrees completely, 48 mA
simulated against 245 measured. A mis-tuned feedforward leaves a residue about
the size of a correct one, so the ON number alone cannot tell them apart.

The **benefit** — OFF against ON — is diagnostic, because it changes sign:

| model L | sim benefit, 400→1200 | sim benefit, 400→2400 |
|---|---|---|
| 54.3 µH (nominal) | −64% | −70% |
| 27.1 µH | **+91%** | **+25%** |
| 18.1 µH | **+113%** | **+85%** |

Below about 30 µH the feedforward over-corrects and the "correction" makes the
disturbance worse. The bench never does this — it shows −57% and −68%, and
both simulator curves cross the measured values at 54.3 µH. **The bench motor
behaves like a motor whose inductance is the one the firmware already
assumes.** The hypothesis is refuted.

## The rise-time gap was the plotting tool, not the motor

Resolved the same day. `viz.py`'s `_find_step` sets `t = 0` for a hardware
capture, and it was returning the **midpoint crossing** — the instant `iq` is
already half way through the step. A simulator CSV puts its `t = 0` at the
command. So the comparison measured 50%→63% on the bench against 0%→63% in
the model, and the bench looked three times faster than it is.

Measured against the index where the firmware actually fires the step
(`STEP_PRE_TICKS`, 60, deterministic because the ISR applies the setpoint, not
SWD):

| | origin = midpoint crossing | origin = firmware's index |
|---|---|---|
| 400→1200, decouple=1 | 67 µs | **200 µs** |
| 400→1200, decouple=0 | 67 µs | 167 µs |
| 400→2400, decouple=1 | 100 µs | **200 µs** |
| 400→2400, decouple=0 | 100 µs | **200 µs** |
| simulator | | **200 µs** |

**There is no rise-time discrepancy. The model's current-loop dynamics match
the bench.** The claim that hardware was 3× faster is withdrawn.

The offset was a consistent ~4 samples (133 µs) on every capture, which is why
it never looked like noise. `_find_step`'s docstring already said the
firmware's index "is the answer" and searching is the fallback for older
captures; the code had it the other way round, searching first and using
`STEP_PRE_TICKS` only when it could find no step at all. Fixed to match the
documented intent, with a fallback that walks back from the crossing to where
the signal left its pre-step level — the command instant is the departure, not
the half-way point.

Two tool bugs in one session, both the same shape: a docstring describing the
correct behaviour over code doing something else. Worth a habit of checking
the two against each other in this repo.

## Phase 1b: the model will not reproduce the failure it is meant to fix

Attempted, and stopped short of a firmware change on purpose.

prompt.md section A says the decoupling stays off because it costs 6x peak
current on a reversal at terminal speed — 9326 mA against 1489 mA — and that
the cause is `omega_e` arriving from the position loop through an ~8 Hz filter,
20 ms behind. The prescribed fix is a second filter on the *same* encoder
differences, faster, used only for the feedforward.

Before writing that, the simulator had to be able to see the problem. It could
not: `sim.h` handed `FOC_Update` the model's exact instantaneous `omega_m`, so
the feedforward was perfect by construction and the failure was absent by
construction. That is now fixed — the model runs position.c's path step for
step, differencing the **quantised** encoder count at `POS_DECIM` and
low-passing it with the same EMA, so both the lag and the quantisation noise
are present. A `reversal` scenario was added: spin up free, then command the
opposite current and let the rotor decelerate through zero.

It reproduces the operating point almost exactly:

| | model | bench |
|---|---|---|
| peak \|i\|, reversal, decouple **off** | 1496 mA | 1489 mA |

**And it does not reproduce the failure.** With decoupling on, at the shipped
8 Hz corner, peak current is 1464 mA — no penalty at all, against the bench's
9326 mA. Sweeping the corner changes nothing:

| omega_e corner | peak \|i\| | peak \|id\| |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Hz (shipped) | 1464 mA | 144 mA |
| 16 / 32 / 48 Hz | ~1477 mA | ~143 mA |
| 80 Hz | 1475 mA | 143 mA |
| 159 Hz | 1471 mA | 144 mA |
| ideal, zero lag | 1502 mA | 140 mA |
| decoupling off | 1496 mA | 380 mA |

Checked at 1000, 2000 and 4000 mA, up to 86% of the window voltage-saturated.
Everywhere: decoupling **helps** the d axis by roughly 3x and costs no current,
and the corner is irrelevant from 8 Hz to perfect.

### Why, and what it implies

The drive is voltage-saturated for 40–86% of a reversal at terminal speed. The
vector limiter therefore clips the total command, and a clipped command cannot
carry an extra 0.86 V of feedforward error into the winding. prompt.md's
arithmetic — 320 rad/s of lag error, times lambda_m, across 85 mOhm, equals
10.1 A — assumes those volts are applied. A saturated drive cannot apply them.
The plan states the saturation itself two sentences earlier ("no headroom to
correct it: `w_e·lambda_m/Vbus` is 0.250 against a `vmax` of 0.250") without
reconciling the two.

So one of these is true, and the measurement to distinguish them has not been
made:

1. the model is missing whatever mechanism the bench has — bus sag under load,
   integrator behaviour on desaturation, or the demo reaching a speed and a
   command profile this scenario does not; or
2. the 9326 mA is real but its cause is not `omega_e` lag, in which case a
   faster `omega_e` fixes nothing.

**No firmware change was made.** Writing the second filter now would be
shipping a correction for a mechanism that the only model able to test it says
does not produce the effect — which is rule 8 of the work plan, and the plan
names `f->decouple` and `f->dtc_pm` as the two corrections already shipped on
reasoning alone that made the bench worse. The next step is to re-measure the
9326 mA with enough instrumentation to identify the mechanism: capture `vmag`
against `vmax`, the integrator states, and Vbus through a demo reversal.

### Measured on the bench afterwards: three answers, and the sampling rate decided which

The model's silence turned out to be the model's, not the machine's. Captured
with `step_trace.sh` driving a +1000 -> -1000 mA reversal, trace decimated to
hold the whole 410 ms event.

**First attempt reproduced nothing** — 1224 mA off against 1421 mA on, 1.16x,
which reads as a pass. It was not. The rotor was at 83% of terminal speed, so
`w_e·lambda_m/Vbus` was 0.207 against a `vmax` of 0.250 and the loop still had
17% of its voltage in hand to correct with. That is precisely the trap the work
plan records ("1359 vs 1375 mA and looked like a pass, because the motor never
reached the speed where this bites"), and the first measurement walked into it.

With the settle lengthened to 3 s the rotor reaches 1932–1950 rad/s and
`w_e·lambda_m/Vbus` is 0.242–0.244 against 0.250 — the zero-headroom condition
the plan describes. Then:

| flag | rep 1 | rep 2 | rep 3 | rep 4 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decouple **off** | 1164 | 1274 | 1221 | 1251 | **1227 ± 41 mA** |
| decouple **on** | 1267 | 1241 | 1708 | **2829** | **1761 ± 644 mA** |

Peak \|id\| on that worst rep was 2081 mA, so the excursion is d-axis
dominated — the signature of a feedforward error rather than a torque
transient.

Three things this says:

- **The failure is real** and the plan's 9326 mA is credible. Four reps reached
  2829 mA and climbing; a full demo runs many more cycles.
- **It is divergent, not static.** 1267, 1241, 1708, 2829 — it grows rep on
  rep from identical starting conditions. A constant feedforward error would
  not do that. Something accumulates, and the integrator is the obvious
  suspect. That reframes the fix: a faster `omega_e` addresses a static lag
  error, and this does not look static.
- **Sustained saturation is not the mechanism.** The bench sits at `vmax` for
  only 0.2–0.8% of the window even at terminal speed. The simulator, by
  contrast, spends 40–86% of a reversal clipped — so if anything the model is
  *more* protected by the limiter, which is why it shows no penalty. The
  earlier reasoning in this document, that the limiter absorbs the error, is
  the right description of the model and the wrong description of the bench.

Stopped at four reps rather than pushing for a bigger number. The trend was
upward, and `HARDWARE_NOTES` section 10 records that there is no hardware
overcurrent path — the trip is a main-loop poll at ~750 Hz. Deliberately
driving a divergent current excursion against a slow trip is not a measurement
worth the board. No fault latched and `fault_count` stayed 0 throughout.

### That reading was wrong. The decimation was aliasing the transient.

The apparent divergence did not survive instrumentation, and the sequence is
worth recording because each step looked conclusive.

**Integrators do not accumulate.** Captured at both rep boundaries:
`id_integ` entering each rep ran 0.0225, 0.0458, 0.0064, -0.0076, -0.0049,
0.0003 — bounded, no trend, nothing carried between reps. Headroom was
0.006–0.009 on every rep including the outliers. Neither hypothesis survived.

`iq_integ` did show the feedforward working exactly as intended: ~0.237 with
decoupling off, where the integrator has to supply the back-EMF term itself,
against ~0.000 with it on.

**Then the peak turned out to be at t = 4 ms — the step itself**, not the
deceleration or the speed zero-crossing. At `decim = 24` that transient is
being sampled one ISR tick in twenty-four, so whether a rep records its true
peak is luck. The "outliers" were the reps whose sample happened to land near
it. Every rep had that peak; only some of them measured it.

**Re-captured at full ISR rate**, terminal speed, six reps per arm:

| | peak \|i\| |
|---|---|
| decouple **off** | 2333 ± 253 mA (1864–2601) |
| decouple **on** | 2340 ± 372 mA (1640–2695) |
| ratio | **1.00x** |

Decoupling costs nothing. The spreads overlap almost entirely. The true peak
is ~2.3 A in both arms — nearly double what the decimated capture reported,
which is the measure of how much that aliasing was hiding.

### Where this leaves 1b

The 6x penalty does not reproduce: not at 83% of terminal, not at terminal,
and not with sampling fast enough to see the transient. Nor does the plan's
1489 mA baseline — a direct terminal-speed reversal peaks at ~2.3 A with
everything off, and a measurement that reported 1489 would have to have been
missing the same transient this one nearly missed.

What has NOT been replicated is the demo's exact profile: 0 -> ±0.5 -> ±1.0
with dwells, rather than a direct ±1 A flip. That is the remaining difference
and the only place the 9326 mA can still be hiding.

**Three different answers came out of the same bench in one sitting — 1.16x,
1.43x-and-divergent, and 1.00x — and the only thing that changed was how fast
the capture sampled and how close to terminal the rotor was.** Neither is
visible in the number itself. Any figure quoted for this test needs its
sampling rate and its pre-step `w_e*lambda_m/Vbus` quoted with it, or it means
nothing.


### A weakness this turned up

Enabling the modelled velocity path globally made `run.sh --mutants` stop
catching the anti-windup mutant, which it had been catching by exactly one
test. The suite's anti-windup coverage is thin enough to depend on which
`omega_e` the loop is handed. The modelled path is therefore opt-in — the
`reversal` scenario selects it — and the thin coverage is recorded here rather
than papered over. Strengthening those tests is worth doing before the
anti-windup is touched again, which field weakening will require.

## Phase 1a cannot be validated on this bench

The `delay_comp` A/B measured +38% peak \|id\| with compensation on — which
reads as "the correction is harmful" and means nothing. The difference is
46 mA against noise floors of 29.5 and 46.6 mA on the averaged waveform: about
one noise unit, at 12 reps.

The simulator says how big the effect actually is: **−9 mA, −7%** on peak
\|id\|. Against those floors, a 2σ read needs

    ~514 reps   from the 29.5 mA floor
    ~1282 reps  from the 46.6 mA floor

So this is not an underpowered measurement that more patience fixes — it is
infeasible by this method on this bench. That is the same conclusion prompt.md
already records for 1a ("stands on derivation"), now with a rep count behind
it. The compensation *is* acting: +5.31° of advance applied against +0.00°
with it off.

Anything that wants to measure 1a needs a lower noise floor, not more reps —
a locked rotor, or a disturbance injected at a known frequency and detected
narrowband.

## Three tool bugs found

**`viz.py select()` matched `--hw-key` against any field.** A capture is named
`hw <flag> <rep>`, and the test was `key in t.name.split()`, so `--hw-key 1`
also matched rep 1 of the *other* arm — a 12-rep A/B plotted as 13 captures
with one from the wrong firmware configuration. Exactly the blend the
function's own docstring calls worse than not comparing at all, arriving
silently. Visible in the operating point too: the contaminated average
reported ω_e 1648 rad/s against the simulator's 1670; matching the flag field
alone puts it at 1669. Fixed in `d8b1239`.

**`viz.py _find_step` returned mid-rise as the step instant.** Covered above;
it is what created the phantom rise-time gap, and it shifted the hardware
trace 133 µs late on every comparison figure in this document. All of them
have been regenerated.

**`step_trace.sh` does not restore the flags it toggles.** It safes the power
stage on exit — that part works — but leaves `f->decouple` at whichever arm
ran last. Observed twice today: `decouple` read 1 after both A/B runs, against
a firmware default of 0 (`foc.c:241`). A reset clears it, and nothing here was
measured through a stale flag because each run sets both arms explicitly. The
hazard is what runs *next*: `foc_dash --demo` immediately after a step trace
would spin with decoupling silently enabled, which is the configuration
prompt.md says costs 6× peak current on a reversal. Not fixed — the tool
should snapshot and restore, but that is a change to a motor-driving script
and wants its own review.

## Also noted

- `foc.h:381` comments `decouple` as *"(default on)"*; `foc.c:241` sets it to
  `0`. The comment is stale.
- `viz.py live` plots the integer mirrors, which only update while the control
  loop runs. Against a drive sitting in `READY` it draws a flat line from
  whenever the loop last ran — it looks like data. Worth a guard that reads
  `g_foc.updates` twice and refuses rather than plotting a frozen snapshot.
- `CSense_MeasureVdda` runs once at startup and latches. One boot in seven
  read VREFINT 1728 against a stable 1522, putting VDDA at 2890 mV instead of
  3280 and every derived voltage 13% low for the whole session. With
  `vbus_track = 1` that mis-scales the current-loop gains too. `vbus_raw` was
  unchanged across the bad boot, which is what rules out an actual rail sag:
  the ADC is ratiometric, so a real 2.89 V rail would have moved the raw code.
